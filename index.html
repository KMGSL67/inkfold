A notebook that asks for nothing.

Inkfold is a free Windows app for handwritten notes, typed text and PDFs you can write on and fill in. No account, no ads, no paid version. It works without an internet connection.

Go on, write on this page.

Handwriting, typed notes and PDFs usually mean three programs.

Inkfold does all three in one quiet app: a library for your notebooks, pens that behave like pens, paper for every subject, and PDFs you can write on and fill in. If you have used Goodnotes, OneNote or Acrobat, you already know the idea.

What it does

A pen that behaves like a pen.

Ink follows your hand and your pressure. Six kinds of pen stand in the tray, from a fineliner to a brush pen, and each one keeps its own colour and thickness.

Colour when you want it.

Every colour has a name, and you can mix your own. Pick an ink and keep writing. The highlighter has colours of its own.

Write on a PDF. Keep the original.

Open a PDF, highlight it, write in the margin and type into its blanks, then export a finished copy. The file you opened is never changed.

The pen draws. A finger moves the page.

With a pen, the pen writes and a finger pans and zooms. Without one, the mouse and keyboard do everything.

What Inkfold asks of you

Nothing: no price, no account, no sign-in, no ads, no paid tier, no telemetry and no internet connection. Inkfold itself makes no network requests.

A price An account A sign-in Ads A paid tier Telemetry An internet connection

nothing.

Your notes stay on your PC. Inkfold itself never goes online. Windows’ WebView2 component, which displays the app, may contact Microsoft on its own, as it does for other Windows apps. WebView2 can also keep crash reports on your PC, which Windows may send to Microsoft depending on your diagnostic-data settings, but Inkfold itself sends nothing. Notes are saved on your own computer, and Inkfold puts a backup of all of them into your Documents folder once a day. If you sync that folder with OneDrive or another service, that is your service at work, not Inkfold.

Free means free. No ads, no paid tier, nothing to unlock later. Free for private use and free for commercial use.

About the Windows notice

The first time you open the installer, Windows SmartScreen shows a box that says “Windows protected your PC”. It appears because the installer is not code-signed. A signing certificate costs money every year, and Inkfold is free.

SmartScreen is a sensible check, and it is right to stop and read it. If you downloaded the installer from the Inkfold releases page on GitHub, choose More info, then Run anyway.

If the file came from anywhere else, do not run it. Delete it and download it again from the releases page.
